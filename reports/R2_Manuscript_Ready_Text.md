## 1. Methods additions

### 1.1 Cell annotation and lineage re-annotation (Discovery cohort)

> Raw count matrices for the six GSE311084 samples were generated with EmptyDrops-based Cell Ranger matrices. After per-sample quality control, 57,476 cells were retained. An initial per-sample clustering and marker-score annotation against a 13-lineage panel was used only as a preliminary quality check. All cells were then integrated for joint analysis and re-annotated at the cluster level by supportive-marker counts across nine lineages (chondrocyte, fibroblast/stromal, endothelial, monocyte, macrophage, T cell, mast cell, osteoclast, neutrophil). Clusters with chondrocyte marker support and no fibroblast/stromal support were called cartilage-lineage; clusters co-expressing chondrocyte and fibroblast/stromal markers were called cartilage-fibrocartilage-candidate; one cluster of 166 cells (7 and 159 cells in two ONFH donors) with dominant macrophage marker support was excluded. The final discovery set therefore comprised 57,310 cartilage-lineage cells (99.7% of quality-controlled cells), all of which retained canonical chondrocyte marker expression (SOX9, ACAN, COL2A1 detected in 53.6%, 75.5% and 60.5% of Fibrocartilage-dominant cells, respectively). Per-cell lineage calls and program assignments are provided in the supplementary tables, and a barcode-level provenance audit reconciles every analysis stage (supplementary Note).

### 1.2 Doublet sensitivity analysis

> To assess whether predicted doublets influenced the Fibrocartilage program, we compared doublet fractions between Fibrocartilage-dominant cells (`dominant_program = "Fibrocartilage"`, n = 7,149) and other cartilage-lineage cells (`lineage_call = "cartilage_lineage"` excluding Fibrocartilage-dominant cells, n = 29,899) called by scDblFinder. Because these two groups differ almost completely in disease composition (99.8% ONFH vs 99.6% Control cells), cell-level comparisons are reported as descriptive only; the primary assessment is at the donor level (paired Wilcoxon test across six donors) and against the condition-balanced Fibrocartilage-candidate group (n = 20,262). Donor-level program scores (AddModuleScore, primary; UCell, secondary) were recomputed before and after removal of predicted doublets and compared between conditions (Welch t-test, Cohen's d; exact permutation over all C(6,3) = 20 donor-label splits; Monte-Carlo permutation with 100,000 draws, seed 42).

### 1.3 External-cohort specificity statistics

> Specificity of the frozen Fibrocartilage signature in the external cohorts was assessed against 1,000 expression-matched random gene sets (deciles of mean expression; seed 42). Two statistics were used, each compared with a null generated by the identical rule: (i) the ssGSEA disease effect (mean score difference, disease minus control), tested against the same statistic computed on random sets (two-sided empirical p with add-one correction); and (ii) the directional concordance rate (fraction of signature genes whose cohort log-fold-change sign matches the frozen per-gene direction), tested against a symmetric null in which the same direction pattern is assigned to matched random sets, and against direction-label permutation within the signature. Because the four signatures differ in size (23–200 genes), raw ssGSEA effect magnitudes were not compared directly; we report null-standardized effects (z) and Cohen's d from per-sample scores. Empirical significance against random sets, disease-vs-control t-tests, and relative specificity versus generic programs are reported as distinct quantities.

### 1.4 Meta-analysis

> Disease effects for each signature in the two external cohorts (raw mean differences of ssGSEA scores) were combined with a DerSimonian-Laird random-effects model (metafor). Standard errors were recovered from the per-cohort t-statistics. Because pooling two studies provides only one degree of freedom for heterogeneity, τ² and I² are reported for completeness but not interpreted substantively; a standardized (Hedges g) sensitivity analysis and a descriptive synthesis (per-cohort effects, confidence intervals, and direction concordance) accompany the pooled estimates.

## 2. Results additions

### 2.1 Annotation provenance and program composition

> Of 57,476 quality-controlled cells, 166 cells forming a macrophage-marker cluster were excluded, leaving 57,310 cartilage-lineage cells from six donors (Control 31,188; ONFH 26,122). Fibrocartilage-dominant cells (n = 7,149) were almost entirely ONFH-derived (99.8%), whereas other cartilage-lineage cells were almost entirely Control-derived (99.6%); Fibrocartilage-dominant cells co-expressed chondrocyte markers (SOX9/ACAN/COL2A1) and fibrocartilage extracellular-matrix genes, consistent with a fibrocartilage-like state of cartilage-lineage cells rather than a distinct non-cartilage lineage.

### 2.2 Doublet sensitivity

> Predicted doublets were not enriched in Fibrocartilage-dominant cells (8.1% vs 11.1% in other cartilage-lineage cells; 8.1% vs 8.9% in the condition-balanced Fibrocartilage-candidate group). The cell-level difference was confounded by disease composition and was not significant at the donor level (paired Wilcoxon p = 0.84). Removing predicted doublets left the donor-level Fibrocartilage difference essentially unchanged (AddModuleScore: 0.556 ± 0.141 vs 1.830 ± 0.267 before, and 0.555 ± 0.139 vs 1.822 ± 0.269 after removal; Welch p = 0.0051 and 0.0054; Cohen's d = 5.97 and 5.92; UCell: p = 0.0094 and 0.0085). With three donors per group, the minimum attainable two-sided exact permutation p is 0.10 (observed one-sided 0.05; Monte-Carlo two-sided ≈ 0.10).

### 2.3 External specificity (revised statistics)

> In GSE74089, the ssGSEA disease effect of the Fibrocartilage signature (0.400) exceeded all 1,000 expression-matched random sets (empirical p = 0.001; standardized z = 5.37), and 22 of 23 signature genes were up-regulated in disease (concordance 0.957 vs symmetric null 0.577, p = 0.001). In GSE345158 the direction of the effect was concordant but the case-control difference was not significant (effect 0.122; t-test p = 0.253; Cohen's d = 0.90), although the effect was large relative to expression-matched random sets (empirical p = 0.002; z = 4.64). The directional-concordance signal reflects coordinated up-regulation of the signature genes in disease; the single gene with a frozen down-direction (DCN) was not reproduced in either cohort. Generic ECM and EMT programs showed larger disease effects than the Fibrocartilage signature in GSE74089 (Cohen's d 14.4 and 9.5 vs 6.6) but not in GSE345158 (0.13 and −0.22 vs 0.90), so the Fibrocartilage signature should be regarded as one of several extracellular-matrix programs activated in ONFH, with cohort-specific prominence rather than universal specificity.

### 2.4 Pooled external evidence

> Pooling the two external cohorts with a DerSimonian-Laird random-effects model gave a combined Fibrocartilage effect of 0.197 (95% CI −0.042 to 0.436; p = 0.107) with high between-study heterogeneity (I² = 93%); with only two cohorts this heterogeneity estimate is unstable, and the pooled test should be interpreted descriptively. Both cohorts showed effects in the same direction, with the magnitude larger in GSE74089 (0.315; Hedges g = 5.7) than in GSE345158 (0.071; g = 0.84).

## 3. Discussion additions

> Our annotation audit clarified that the discovery cohort consists of cartilage-lineage cells defined by marker-based re-annotation of an integrated object; cells co-expressing chondrocyte and fibroblast/stromal markers were retained as cartilage-fibrocartilage-candidate, which matches the biology of fibrocartilage, a tissue whose extracellular matrix contains both collagen-I-rich and aggrecan-rich components. The Fibrocartilage program therefore represents a state of cartilage-lineage cells, not a contaminating fibroblast population.
>
> The external-cohort analyses illustrate three distinct questions that are easily conflated: whether a signature's disease effect exceeds what is expected for expression-matched random gene sets (empirical specificity), whether disease and control samples differ significantly (case-control testing), and whether the signature is more disease-responsive than generic extracellular-matrix programs (relative specificity). The Fibrocartilage signature is empirically specific in both external cohorts, significantly increased in GSE74089, and directionally concordant but not individually significant in GSE345158; it is not uniformly more disease-responsive than generic ECM programs. These results support a conserved fibrocartilage-remodeling tendency with cohort-dependent magnitude, rather than a universally dominant program.
>
> Doublet detection provides an additional safeguard: predicted doublets were not enriched in Fibrocartilage-dominant cells, and removing them did not attenuate the donor-level difference, indicating that the program is not an artifact of multiplet capture.

## 4. Limitations (for the Limitations paragraph)

> Several limitations should be noted. First, the discovery cohort comprises three donors per group; at n = 3 vs 3 the minimum two-sided exact permutation p is 0.10, and all donor-level parametric tests rest on normality assumptions. Second, per-cell preliminary annotations from the per-sample phase of the analysis are not recoverable at barcode level; the annotation provenance is documented through cluster-level records and exact count reconciliation. Third, the external cohorts are small (4 vs 4 and 5 vs 4), and with two cohorts the random-effects heterogeneity estimate is unstable; pooled estimates are reported descriptively. Fourth, the directional-concordance analysis shows that the signature behaves as a coordinated up-regulated program in disease; the single frozen down-direction gene (DCN) was not reproduced externally. Fifth, scDblFinder classifications are computational predictions rather than experimental ground truth. Finally, ssGSEA scores are platform-normalized but not variance-homogenized, which is why standardized effect sizes accompany all cross-cohort comparisons.

2. Wherever the doublet analysis is cited, cite the donor-level paired test (p = 0.84) and the before/after score comparison; quote cell-level Fisher values only as descriptive statistics with the composition caveat.
3. Wherever the external validation is cited, do not present the random-set empirical p as a case-control p-value; state that GSE345158 alone is not significant at case-control level (p = 0.253).
4. Do not describe the pooled random-effects estimate as statistically significant; report it descriptively with the per-cohort effects.
5. Do not claim reproduction of the full frozen direction pattern externally; the concordance reflects coordinated up-regulation (DCN's frozen down-direction was not reproduced).
